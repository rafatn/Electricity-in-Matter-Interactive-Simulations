Visual Communication over Power Line

Engineering Whitepaper – Visual Media Bus Architecture

1. Executive Summary

This document presents a system for real-time transmission of visual media (video and audio) over standard electrical power lines. The proposed architecture transforms the power grid into a dedicated visual communication bus, without reliance on IP networking, Ethernet, or HDMI cabling.

Low Latency No IP Stack Plug & View

2. Concept Overview

Conventional power lines deliver electrical energy at 230V / 50Hz. The proposed system introduces an additional high-frequency carrier superimposed on the same conductors, enabling bidirectional visual communication.

  • The power signal remains unmodified
  • Visual data is spectrally modulated at higher frequencies
  • Each electrical outlet functions as a media transmitter and receiver

3. Physical Bandwidth Analysis

Assumptions

  • Approved frequency band: 1–30 MHz
  • Effective usable bandwidth: ~20 MHz
  • Average SNR: 20–30 dB
  • Modulation: OFDM with adaptive QAM

4. SNR vs Bitrate Behavior

The achievable bitrate increases logarithmically with SNR, similar to DSL and DOCSIS systems. Adaptive modulation ensures robustness under varying noise conditions.

6. Latency Analysis

  • Low-latency video encoding: 10–20 ms
  • Modulation and power line propagation: 1–3 ms
  • Decoding and rendering: 10–20 ms

Total end-to-end latency: ≈ 25–40 ms

8. Regulatory Considerations

  • CENELEC Band A (3–95 kHz): Utility control
  • Band B (95–125 kHz): Consumer devices
  • Band C/D (125 kHz–30 MHz): Communication

The proposed system operates within Band C/D and applies dynamic spectral notching to avoid radio interference.

9. Hardware Architecture (FPGA / SoC)

  • Low-latency video encoder (H.265 / AV1)
  • Custom media packetization (non-IP)
  • OFDM modulator and demodulator
  • Isolated analog front-end and coupling network
  • Hardware-assisted encryption

Target platforms include FPGA-based SoCs such as: Xilinx Zynq, Intel Cyclone V, or equivalent.

10. Conclusion

Electrical power lines can serve as a reliable and efficient medium for real-time visual communication. The proposed architecture introduces a new category of visual media transport that combines low latency, infrastructure independence, and plug-and-play deployment.
